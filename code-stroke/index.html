The 15 NIHSS items

Total 0 to 42. Give the items in order, record the first effort, and score what the patient does, not what you think they could do.

Ground rules

  • Score each item right after testing it; don't go back and change scores.
  • Don't coach. Repeat requests only where the item allows it.
  • Score the first attempt unless the item says otherwise.
  • Score every item. UN (untestable) only for amputation or joint fusion (motor, ataxia) or a physical barrier to speech (dysarthria). UN adds nothing to the total.
  • A patient in coma (1a = 3) has several items scored automatically; see the notes.

Reperfusion decisions in this simulator

  • Thrombolysis (tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg, max 25 mg, or alteplase): within 4.5 h of last known well for a disabling deficit, whatever the NIHSS. Non-disabling deficits get dual antiplatelet therapy instead.
  • Within 4.5 h: do not wait for CTA or CT perfusion before thrombolysis (Class 1).
  • CT perfusion-guided thrombolysis (Class 2a): patients who are not thrombectomy candidates and either woke with symptoms within 9 h of the midpoint of sleep or are 4.5–9 h from last known well, with salvageable penumbra on automated perfusion. EXTEND criteria: core (rCBF <30%) under 70 mL, Tmax >6 s to core ratio over 1.2, mismatch volume over 10 mL.
  • LVO at 4.5–24 h without thrombectomy access (Class 2b): perfusion-selected thrombolysis may be beneficial (TRACE-III); with prompt thrombectomy it added nothing (TIMELESS).
  • Unknown onset with MRI: DWI lesion under one-third of the MCA territory without marked FLAIR change, within 4.5 h of symptom recognition (WAKE-UP, Class 2a).
  • Thrombectomy, 0–6 h: ICA or M1 occlusion with NIHSS 6 or more, ASPECTS 3–10 and prestroke mRS 0–1.
  • Thrombectomy, 6–24 h: small core with clinical-imaging mismatch (DAWN, DEFUSE 3), or ASPECTS 3–5 if under 80 with no significant mass effect (large-core trials).
  • Not recommended: thrombectomy for nondominant or codominant M2 and more distal occlusions, and reperfusion beyond 24 h outside trials.
  • Recent DOAC use is a relative contraindication to thrombolysis that needs individual assessment.
  • Source: Prabhakaran et al. 2026 Guideline for the Early Management of Patients With Acute Ischemic Stroke (AHA/ASA), Stroke 2026, doi:10.1161/STR.0000000000000513. Teaching summary only; follow local protocols.
